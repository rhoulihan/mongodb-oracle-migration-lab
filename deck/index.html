Three doors and one room

Moving the bytes is the easy part. Pick the door.

Here's the map. Three strategies, plotted on two axes: downtime and reversibility. Age out is near-zero downtime but slow, and the legacy system becomes your archive. Dump and restore is planned downtime, fast, and a one-way door once writes resume. Blue/green is near-zero downtime and reversible, with the most moving parts.

Optimization is not a fourth strategy. It's the room on the other side of every door, and each door gives you a different amount of time and freedom to remodel once you're in it.

Timing: five minutes here, then straight into discovery.
Segment 1 · Discovery 0:05 · 10 min

Inventory the workload, not just the data

Which operators, which types, which data even needs to move.

Figure: workload inventory and decision factors
Discovery decides the door. Collections and sizes matter, but the questions that pick a strategy are about life cycle and features. How much of this data is transient? Carts, sessions and event logs with a TTL index never need migrating. Which operators does the app actually use? Which BSON types are sitting in there that have no Oracle JSON scalar?

Demo 1 answers the last two on our telecom order system: a scripted operator scan against the compatibility matrix, and a per-field type census that finds the landmines before they find you.
Segment 2 · Strategy A 0:15 · 12 min

Age out: move only what matters

New writes land on Oracle. Old data expires where it lives.

Figure: cutoff router, entity data moved, event data ageing out
Strategy A. From day N every new write goes to Oracle. You migrate only the long-lived entity data that new records point at: subscribers, accounts, the catalog. Everything else stays in MongoDB until its retention window runs out.

The cost is a router in the app tier that sends each read to the right side of the cutoff, and a second database you operate for the life of the window. Demo 2 shows the router and shows carts expiring in MongoDB, data that never needed to move.
Segment 3 · Strategy B 0:27 · 13 min

Dump and restore in a window

Stop writes, move everything, prove it, then flip.

Figure: maintenance window timeline and validation gate
Strategy B is the simplest to explain and the hardest to undo. Open the window, stop writes, mongodump, mongorestore into Oracle, validate, swap the connection string, restart. Once writes land on Oracle, the door is locked behind you.

Equal document counts prove nothing. The validation gate is a type differential per field and a replay of the app's own predicates on both sides. Demo 3 does exactly that, live, and the differential catches a field that reads back identical but fails a type check.
Segment 4 · Strategy C 0:40 · 14 min

Blue/green with a way back

Sync live, flip with no downtime, keep the fallback path open.

Figure: forward sync, cutover, reverse sync for fallback
Strategy C keeps both systems live. A replicator reads the MongoDB change stream and applies every change to Oracle. When lag is near zero, you flip the app. Then you reverse the replicator so changes on Oracle flow back to MongoDB, and the old system stays a real fallback.

In production that replicator is Oracle GoldenGate. On this laptop it's a small stand-in we built, and the console labels it that way. Demo 4 proves no lost and no duplicated writes across the flip.

The room behind every door

Model the domain. Project the access.

Figure: canonical form, projections, projected shapes
This is the part most migrations skip. You moved the data. Now the question is whether the model you brought along still makes sense on an engine that can join, index and plan across shapes.

Three demos. Demo 5 is a real-time top-N counter: the embedded document pays on every write, the lookup pipeline pays on every read, and the converged model pays a cheap join that the optimizer plans. Demo 6 runs a hard pipeline and the same question as $sql, results asserted equal before any timing. Demo 7 shows ETags stopping a silent lost update between two agents.
Segment 6 · Recap 1:20 · 10 min

Which door fits your workload?

The decision matrix, then your questions.

Figure: decision matrix
Back to the matrix from discovery. Short data life cycle pushes you to age out. A downtime window that fits your data volume pushes you to dump and restore. No tolerance for downtime, or a mandatory rollback plan, pushes you to blue/green. Heavy aggregation pain today works with any door, through the optimization track.
